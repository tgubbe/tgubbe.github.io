Bayesian spatial modeling · R · STAT coursework

Bayesian Spatial Probit Models

What puts a county in China's national top-100 ranking, and does a county's status depend on its neighbors? Using panel data on 55 counties in Jiangsu province from 2010 to 2020 (605 county-years), I built two Bayesian spatial probit models with MCMC sampling: a linear model and a varying coefficient model that uses regression splines to let the baseline shift over time.

  • Screened predictors with correlation and VIF checks, keeping GDP, population and two financial indexes (cy, jr), all standardized.
  • Every predictor has a positive effect with a 95% credible interval that excludes zero.
  • Spatial correlation ρ = 0.619: top-ranked counties cluster together, so a county's neighbors help predict its own ranking.
  • The varying coefficient model fit better, with a lower DIC (680.78 vs. 691.08) and a slightly higher AUC (0.791 vs. 0.783).

Mixed Effects Models for HIV Treatment Data

How does each patient's viral load change during antiviral treatment? Using AIDS clinical trial data (48 patients, 361 measurements, first 90 days), I fit four mixed effects models, two linear and two nonlinear with exponential decay, then estimated each patient's own trajectory with best linear unbiased predictors (BLUPs).

  • The nonlinear random coefficient model (NLRC) fit best by a wide margin, with an AIC of 494.95 against 729.20 for the linear mixed model.
  • The linear models drew jagged lines through the data, while exponential decay curves matched how viral load actually falls.
  • Across 328 patient-visits, NLRC gave the closest prediction 50.3% of the time, and the two nonlinear models together 77.4%.
  • Patient-level BLUPs show how far each person sits from the average response, which helps clinicians judge individual treatment progress.

Classification · R · STAT coursework

Predicting Prostate Cancer Spread

Can routine test results flag patients whose tumor has penetrated the prostatic capsule? Using 375 patients from an Ohio State University Comprehensive Cancer Center study, I built a logistic regression model with stepwise AIC selection, checked it with deviance residuals, Cook's distance and a Hosmer-Lemeshow test, then tuned its decision cutoff for clinical use.

  • Three predictors made the final model: digital rectal exam result, PSA level and Gleason score. Age, race and all interaction terms dropped out.
  • A nodule on the right lobe raises the odds of capsule penetration 4.7×; each extra Gleason point raises them 2.7×.
  • AUC of 0.817. Lowering the cutoff from 0.50 to 0.39 raised sensitivity from 61% to 77%, because a missed case costs more than an extra follow-up exam.

Linear regression · R · STAT coursework

Predicting Municipal Expenditures for New Housing

Two New York villages, Monroe and Warwick, are planning new housing projects and need to know how much spending per resident to expect before deciding on tax changes. Using 914 New York municipalities, I log-transformed heavily skewed variables, focused on high-population, high-density areas, and built a regression model to forecast each village's spending.

  • Chose seven predictors with stepwise AIC, cross-checked against BIC and Mallows' Cp best-subset searches, and checked multicollinearity with VIF.
  • Wealth, population, income, density, growth rate and the share of revenue from state and federal grants all matter (adjusted R² = 0.63).
  • Warwick's spending is forecast to climb from $265 to $302 per person by 2025, while Monroe's stays near $240. Warwick may need new funding; Monroe likely won't.

Classification · Python & R

Predicting Credit Card Default

Can a bank tell which clients will miss a payment? Using 30,000 credit card clients from the UCI Machine Learning Repository, I cleaned and scaled the data, trimmed extreme credit-limit outliers (about 2% of rows), and compared three classifiers on a held-out test set of 8,799 clients.

  • All three models land near 81–82% overall accuracy, but that number hides the hard part.
  • Logistic regression catches only about 1 in 4 actual defaulters. Random forest and the neural network catch closer to 2 in 5.
  • A follow-up in R tuned the logistic threshold to trade precision for recall, and recommends adding credit score and utilization data.

Text analysis · Python / NLTK

Readability of Dickens and Hawthorne

Two 19th-century novelists writing in the same era, one English and one American. Do their books read differently? I built a corpus of 11 works (over 1.6 million words) with the GutenTag corpus builder and scored each with three readability formulas, then looked at what drives the gap.

  • Every Hawthorne work scores harder than every Dickens work on all three indexes (ARI, Coleman-Liau, Flesch-Kincaid).
  • Dickens leans on short words; his word-length distribution peaks at 1 character, Hawthorne's at 3.
  • Average sentence length looks like the strongest driver: about 15–19 words per sentence for Dickens versus 22–30 for Hawthorne.

Exploratory analysis · Python

Superstore Sales & Profit EDA

A retailer's four years of orders (9,994 rows, 2014–2017, from Kaggle), explored to inform marketing decisions about products, customers and regions. I cleaned the data, split dates into month and year, and broke sales and profit down by state, region, category and sub-category.

  • High sales don't guarantee profit: Texas ranks 3rd in sales but misses the top 20 states for profit.
  • Technology is the most profitable category; furniture sells a lot but earns little.
  • Sales climb every year toward the holiday season, and profits grew steadier by 2017.
